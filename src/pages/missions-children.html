Two directions, one mission

Missions for Children runs both ways. We take the Gospel to children in the places we serve, and we help the children already among us grow up knowing they have a part to play.

Most ministries do one of these. A child in Ghana is treated as someone to be reached, and a child in Colorado Springs is treated as someone to be entertained until they are old enough to matter. We think both of those are a mistake.

A seven-year-old can understand the Gospel. A seven-year-old can pray for a country by name, give from money that is genuinely theirs, and tell a friend at school what they believe. That is not preparation for mission. It is mission.

To children

Reaching children where we serve

Working through the local church, never around it, and never taking a child out of the care of the adults responsible for them.

  • Children's Bible clubs run by local believers who stay
  • Age-appropriate Gospel material in the languages children think in
  • School and community outreach where a local church is invited
  • Follow-up that connects a child to a congregation, not to a visiting team
  • Practical help where poverty is what keeps a child from learning
With children

Raising children who go

Helping the children of our own assemblies take a genuine part now, at the level they are actually at.

  • Praying for real people and real places, by name
  • Giving out of their own money, and deciding where it goes
  • Writing to and hearing back from people on the field
  • Serving locally alongside their parents, not instead of them
  • Travelling with family as they grow old enough to serve well

What It Looks Like at Each Age

Mission for a child should fit the child. These are the steps we build around, and no step assumes the one before it was perfect.

  • 0 to 4

    Hearing the story

    Bible stories told properly, a one-sentence prayer they can say themselves, and the ordinary experience of being welcome among God's people.

  • 5 to 8

    Praying by name

    A country, a family, a missionary they can picture. Giving something small that costs them something real. Learning that people far away are people, not projects.

  • 9 to 12

    Taking part

    Writing to someone on the field and getting a reply. Helping at a local outreach. Learning enough about a place to explain it to someone else. Beginning to tell friends what they believe.

  • 13+

    Serving and going

    Real responsibility on local outreach, and travelling with family or a properly supervised team. Preparing for the day the decision is entirely theirs.

Safeguarding Comes First

Any ministry that works with children has to be judged on this before anything else, so we will be plain about it.

Every adult in a position of trust with children completes screening, references, and safeguarding orientation before they begin. Ministry with children happens in view of other adults. Concerns go to the responsible leader promptly, and to the statutory authorities where the law requires it. We would rather lose face than lose a child.

Read our full safeguarding commitments

What we will never do

  • Never one-to-one, out of sight. No adult is alone and unobserved with a child, on any field, for any reason.
  • Never separate a child from their family or their guardians, or make private arrangements with a household.
  • Never photograph or publish a child without the written consent of a parent or guardian, and never in a way that shows them as pitiful.
  • Never promise what we cannot keep. No visitor offers a child money, sponsorship, a visa, or a return visit that Karis has not agreed.
  • Never place an unvetted volunteer with children, however willing, however short the trip, however well we know them.
