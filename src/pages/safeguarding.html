Ministry involves trust, and trust can be abused. Karis Missions International takes the protection of children, young people, and vulnerable adults seriously, not as a formality, but as a matter of obedience to God and love for people.

Our Commitments

  • People come before reputation. If protecting someone would embarrass the ministry, we protect the person.
  • No one serves unvetted. Anyone in a position of trust with children or vulnerable adults completes screening, references, and safeguarding orientation before they begin.
  • No unsupervised one-to-one access. Ministry with children and young people happens in view of others, with more than one adult present.
  • Consent before any image is used. We do not photograph, film, or publish anyone without informed consent, and never a child without the written consent of a parent or guardian.
  • Concerns are acted on, not managed away. A concern is passed to the responsible leader promptly, and to the statutory authorities where required by law.
  • Boundaries are explicit. Volunteers are told plainly what is and is not appropriate in conduct, communication, physical contact, gifts, and social media.

On the Mission Field

Cross-cultural mission carries additional risk: participants are away from familiar accountability, working with people who may be poor, unwell, or displaced, and often in situations where a visitor holds obvious power. Karis therefore requires that:

  • Every participant completes safeguarding preparation before travelling.
  • Participants work under the direction of local church leadership, never independently.
  • No participant takes a child away from a supervised setting, or makes private arrangements with a family, for any reason.
  • Photography of communities we serve is limited, respectful, consented to, and never used in a way that reduces a person to a fundraising image.
  • Participants do not make promises, of money, sponsorship, visas, or return visits. That Karis has not agreed to keep.

Dignity is not optional. We do not publish photographs or stories that portray people as helpless in order to raise support. The people we serve are not a fundraising instrument, and we will decline material that treats them as one.

Reporting a Concern

If you are worried about the safety or welfare of a child or vulnerable adult in connection with Karis Missions International or any of our assemblies, please tell us. You do not need to be certain, and you do not need proof.

If someone is in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services first. In the United States, call 911. Reporting to Karis is never a substitute for contacting the statutory authorities where a crime may have been committed.

How We Respond

  1. We listen, take the concern seriously, and write down what we are told.
  2. We do not investigate allegations of abuse ourselves; we refer them to the appropriate authorities.
  3. We take immediate steps to make the person safe, including suspending anyone whose involvement is in question, without prejudging the outcome.
  4. We keep the person who raised the concern informed as far as confidentiality allows.
  5. We review what happened afterwards and change our practice where it needs changing.

If We Get It Wrong

If you believe Karis has handled a safeguarding matter badly, tell us directly and ask for the concern to be escalated to the Board. If you would rather raise it outside the ministry, you are entitled to contact the statutory safeguarding authority in your area, and we will not regard that as disloyalty.

This document is a statement of commitments, not a complete policy. A full safeguarding policy, including named safeguarding leads, screening standards, record-keeping, and a code of conduct, is being finalised as part of the ministry's governance work. It will be published here when adopted by the Board. In the meantime, the commitments above are in force and can be relied upon.
